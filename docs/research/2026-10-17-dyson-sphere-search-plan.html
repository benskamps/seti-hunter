01 · The answer

A limit, not a detection.

Every Dyson-sphere candidate so far has turned out to be dust or a galaxy behind the star. Our edge is a sample where that trick mostly can't work.

Recommended first stepOne session: freeze the IR rejection ladder, resolve all 1,133 stars to Gaia IDs, and cross-match with positions moved to each catalogue's date. Then run the ladder on two control sets before any real star is scored: known debris disks (η Crv, Vega, Fomalhaut, ε Eri), and the Project Hephaistos candidates the literature has since eliminated.

Why this sampleThe field's main false positive is a faint dusty galaxy hiding inside WISE's 6–12″ beam. Around nearby, bright stars that galaxy is a few-percent bump, not a 60× excess. The radio half of a joint limit is already being measured by seti-hunter on the same stars.

Expected productA per-star table: the largest waste-heat covering fraction WISE still allows, next to seti-hunter's narrowband radio limit. A short RNAAS note of its own that cites seti-hunter is the recommended form; folding it into seti-hunter's own note is the alternative (D1).

03 · The signatures

Five ways a swarm could show.

Each checked against the physics before it gets a place in the plan. One carries the search; the others support it.

Primary channelcomputed

Mid-infrared waste heat

A shell at 1 AU from the Sun sits at 331–394 K and peaks near 10 µm — right in WISE's 12 and 22 µm bands. A swarm catching just 0.1% of a Sun-like star's light at 300 K adds about twice the star's own 22 µm flux. Energetics are not the problem; calibration, saturation and confusion set the limit. Reflective or very cold (<100 K) swarms escape, so every limit names its 100–600 K blackbody model.

Cross-checklow prior

Dimming without the glow

Light removed must re-emerge. Dimming with no WISE excess means the heat is colder than ~50–100 K or beamed away. Colour-dependent dimming means dust, as at Boyajian's Star. Gaia DR4 epoch photometry would sharpen this; ESA gives no date yet.

Side channelweak here

Anomalous transit shapes

A transit needs alignment: about a 0.5% chance at 1 AU. Over 1,133 stars that is a handful of aligned systems even if every star had a swarm. Keep it in a "weird anything" bin with no limit claimed. TESS coverage of the sample is unchecked.

Identity onlyprobe

Gaia oddities

"Too faint for its parallax" fails mainly through binaries. 270 of 1,117 matched stars have RUWE > 1.4 — a binarity and brightness flag, not a technosignature. Use Gaia for identity, distance and the photosphere.

Independent evidencein hand

Radio, two ways

A swarm need not leak radio, so radio confirms nothing. It does two clean jobs: VLASS (2–4 GHz, 2.5″) catches the background AGN that eliminated candidate G, and seti-hunter's per-star narrowband limits fill the joint table.

04 · The ladder

Every flag goes down five rungs.

Same rules as seti-hunter's radio ladder: fixed order, frozen before the run, the first rung that eliminated each object goes in the Ledger. Objects that can't be tested are Untested, never passed.

  1. Artifact. The excess sits on a WISE artifact or saturation flag, or vanishes when re-measured.AllWISE ccf · ext · qph · unWISE · CatWISE2020
  2. Confusion. The infrared centroid is offset from the Gaia position, or another optical, NIR or radio source sits inside the 22 µm beam.Gaia DR3 · deep imaging (coverage unverified) · VLASS
  3. Natural dust. A known debris disk, silicate feature, resolved disk, or signs of youth.Literature · Spitzer & Herschel archives
  4. Star class. Giants, AGB stars and Be stars glow in the infrared on their own. The sample includes Arcturus, Aldebaran and Kochab.Gaia CMD · SIMBAD spectral type
  5. Not re-found. The excess disagrees between AllWISE, unWISE and NEOWISE epochs, or with Spitzer.NEOWISE single exposures, 2013–2024
  6. Survivor. Held privately, fitted as sphere versus dust, second reviewer (Codex, graded). Only then a JWST or ALMA proposal.

Confusion (②) is checked before dust (③) on purpose: a known disk star can also have a galaxy behind it, and the instrument problem should be recorded before the literature explains it away.

Positive controls — must flag, then be eliminated at ③
  • η Crv — verified warm disk; flags in the dry run at W3−W4 = +0.56 probe
  • ε Eri — W3−W4 = +0.34 probe
  • Vega, Fomalhaut — classic disks not opened

A run that misses these is invalid.

External test set — must be eliminated at ① or ②

The Hephaistos candidates A–J, run through our own code. D, E and G have known answers. If D or E pass, rung ② is too weak — expected for archival-only checks, and the note says so.

Injection-recovery sets the limits: synthetic swarms added to each star's real photometry, run through the identical pipeline.

The five dry-run flags are not candidates; they are what the ladder is for. η Crv is a textbook warm debris disk, which shows the machinery sees real dust. ζ Lep has interferometric hints of inner material unverified. γ Oph, 73 Her and BD−07 699 are unexamined, and BD−07 699 sits on a diffraction-spike flag — a likely rung ① elimination.

Three snags, each with a fix

  1. The CSV positions can't be matched directly. They are coarse and mix epochs. GJ 1002 moves ~2″ a year; its 2MASS counterpart sits 40.8″ from the listed position, and a naive 30″ search returns two unrelated faint stars. probeResolve each star to a Gaia ID, move its position to each catalogue's date, then match.
  2. The short WISE bands are saturated. 1,076 of 1,115 matched stars, and 2MASS fails for the brightest (Sirius's row reads J = H = K = −1.39). probeModel the photosphere from Gaia, and use the W3−W4 colour, which needs no model and covers 100–300 K swarms. 600 K swarms depend on the photosphere model, the main systematic.
  3. Bright stars, giants and multiples. 16 stars are too bright for Gaia, the sample includes giants, and Sirius returns two artifact-flagged rows. probeA separate bright/evolved class through rung ④, reported as Untested where the photometry can't support a test.
Feasibleseconds of compute · the work is identity + photosphere

What the joint limit is, and isn't

Putting the two limits side by side tightens neither. Its value is a per-star statement: around star X, no 100–600 K swarm above covering fraction f, and no narrowband transmitter above EIRP E during the 2016–19 observations. I found no prior paper doing this for the Breakthrough Listen sample in two searches — probable, not proven, until an ADS check. An optional extension covers ~288,000 field stars inside the Green Bank beams, already catalogued with Gaia IDs and radio limits [33].

Boundary

This plan does not constrain reflective swarms, swarms colder than ~100 K, or near-complete spheres that would have dimmed a star out of a sample chosen for being bright. Our per-star covering fraction is not the same quantity as Hephaistos I's population fraction; quoting both needs care. No Codex pressure-test was run — recommended before the ladder is frozen.

07 · Your calls

Decisions that are Ben's.

Nothing below blocks the first session; D1 and D2 shape everything after it.

  1. Shape. A module inside seti-hunter with its own short note recommended, a separate project, or one section of seti-hunter's note.
  2. Scope. The 1,133 stars first recommended; optionally the ~288,000 field stars (needs field-star radio limits too). Not a five-million-star scan.
  3. Dashboard. An IR funnel and Ledger column on brokenbranch.dev/listen/, or keep IR off the live page until the note.
  4. Survivor policy. Reuse seti-hunter's private-review rule word for word recommended.
  5. Timing. Start now in parallel (it only reads seti-hunter's limits), or after seti-hunter's limits milestone. Either way the IR ladder can be frozen first.
  6. Dimming channel. Wait for Gaia DR4 epoch photometry, or drop it.
  7. Wording. Confirm "eliminated" applies to infrared objects exactly as to radio signals. The plan uses it throughout.
