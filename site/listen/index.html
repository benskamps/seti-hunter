seti-hunter · a public survey · Green Bank · 1.1–2.8 GHz

Listening to 1,133 stars for the signals nobody else looked for.

Every star Breakthrough Listen pointed the Green Bank Telescope at for its 2016–2019 survey, searched again for wide, curving, flickering and comb-shaped radio signals. Everything the search flags goes down the same five-rung ladder in public, and this page shows the reason each one was eliminated.

The expected answer is a limit, not a detection. A survivor becomes a request to look again, never a claim.

01

Three ideas carry the page

Three ideas carry the whole page: how the telescope looks, what a real signal’s fingerprint is, and the ladder every flagged signal climbs down.

i What a cadence is

Each five-minute look at the star alternates with a look somewhere else. A signal from the star should follow the telescope: there in every ON, gone in every OFF.

ii The three fingerprints

Bar height is signal strength in each of the six scans; the dashed line is the detection threshold. Only the first pattern survives rungs 1 and 2. Fingerprints here are drawn as a guide, not data.

iii The ladder, in order

  1. 1Present in any OFF scan
  2. 2Missing from any ON scan
  3. 3An instrument artifact: a coarse-channel spike or edge
  4. 4Inside a known human transmitter band
  5. 5Also seen around another star
  6. ✓Passes all five → private review

Every flagged signal goes down the same rungs, frozen before the run. The page records the first rung that eliminated it.

    07

    Where the claims stop

    BOUNDARY

    Covered

    • Four committed shapes, each with an upper limit measured from planted signals in the real files: wide drifting carriers, fast and curving drifts, intermittent tones, and combs.
    • An open-ended “weird anything” channel that goes down the same ladder, with no limit claimed for it.
    • Drift rates from 0 to about ±95 Hz/s, the edge of the drift bank.
    • L- and S-band (1.1–2.8 GHz) mid-resolution Green Bank files, about 2.86 kHz by 1.07 s, complete six-scan cadences only. Incomplete cadences are counted and listed, not scored.

    Not searched

    • Hz-narrow tones, beyond using them to calibrate. That is the ground of Price et al. 2020, Ma et al. 2023 and Painter et al. 2024.
    • Millisecond pulses: one-second sampling can’t see them.
    • Parkes data, C- and X-band, and any star outside Price 2020’s Green Bank tables.

    An elimination is a reason, not a verdict on the star.

    Four things this page will never show

    • A signal waiting on rung 5. Only a count, changed once a day.
    • A star’s list while anything of it is still pending or under review.
    • The star, frequency or date of a signal under private review. Only the count, and the four-step tracker.
    • Anything the run’s state file holds that isn’t on the published contract. The survey redacts it, and the site’s proxy redacts it again.
